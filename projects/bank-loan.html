Bank Loan Data Report

Project Overview

The workbook tracks a bank's loan book issued through 2021 across branches like Mumbai, Thane, Vashi, Pune, Nagpur, and Kolhapur. Each record captures borrower details (age, gender, occupation, income, home ownership), loan terms (amount, interest rate, installment, DTI), and outcome status (Fully Paid, Charged Off, or Current)

Objective

The apparent goal is to evaluate loan portfolio health and flag risk patterns — specifically, understanding what share of loans turn bad, who is more likely to default, and how loan/borrower attributes (interest rate, DTI, income, loan purpose) relate to repayment outcomes. This supports underwriting and portfolio-risk decisions.

Results & Insights

  • Total loans: 600, with ₹36.4L (3,636,075) disbursed and ₹38.5L (3,854,908) collected back overall — so the book is cash-flow positive.
  • Loan quality: 69.7% Good Loans (418) vs. 30.3% Bad Loans (182) — roughly 1 in 3 loans defaults (Charged Off).
  • Status split: 257 Fully Paid, 182 Charged Off, 161 still Current.
  • Bad loans carry higher interest: Bad loans average 9.97% interest vs. 8.97% for good loans — priced riskier, as expected, but the gap is modest.
  • DTI isn't strongly differentiating: Debt-to-income is nearly identical between good (10.97%) and bad (11.07%) loans — DTI alone doesn't explain defaults well here.
  • Bad loans account for ~31.5% of total loan value (₹11.4L), slightly higher than their 30.3% count share — meaning bad loans skew marginally larger.
  • Loan purpose: Education (131), Medical (112), and Personal (106) are the top reasons for borrowing; Venture and Debt Consolidation follow.
  • Home ownership is fairly evenly split: Rent (203), Own (199), Mortgage (198).
  • Verification status is a red flag area: 305 loans were Not Verified vs. 295 Verified — over half the book isn't income/identity verified, a meaningful risk gap.
  • Branch concentration: Mumbai alone accounts for ~25% of all loans (152 of 600).
  • Borrower tenure: A large cluster (130) have 10+ years employment history, but another large group (75) has under 1 year — a bimodal risk profile.
