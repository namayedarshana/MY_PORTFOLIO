Indian Climate & AQI Analysis

An interactive Power BI dashboard designed to analyze weather conditions and air quality across Indian cities.

Project Overview

This project analyzed 2025 weather and AQI data for 92 Indian cities using Power BI. Compared cities and regions based on AQI, temperature, rainfall, humidity and visibility, and identified patterns such as the hottest, coldest, wettest and most polluted locations.

Objective

  • Analyze weather conditions across Indian cities.
  • Compare air quality between different locations.
  • Create meaningful AQI categories for easier analysis.
  • Identify cities with better and poorer air quality.
  • Build an interactive Power BI dashboard for data-driven exploration.

Dataset

The project used weather and air quality information collected for multiple Indian cities. Weather data was obtained using a weather API and prepared for analysis in Power BI.

Insights

  • Extreme temperature range: Cities span from 46.2°C (Nagpur) to -30.5°C (Drass) Drass and Leh are far colder than the rest of the "coldest" list, standing out as outliers.
  • Inverse temp-humidity cycle: Temperature peaks in May-June, but humidity spikes later in July-September during monsoon. This shows India's pre-monsoon heat gives way to monsoon-driven humidity.
  • Rainfall isn't temperature-driven: Himachal Pradesh gets heavy rainfall (~300mm) despite cooler temps, while warmer states like Kerala and Goa see moderate rainfall. Ladakh has both low temp and low rainfall, showing geography/monsoon exposure matters more than temperature alone
  • Extreme pollution range across cities: AQI spans from 45.60 (Drass) to 230.03 (New Delhi) — nearly 5x difference. New Delhi stands out as the most polluted, driven by heavy vehicle load and industrial activity.
  • Winter is the pollution danger zone: AQI climbs steeply from October to December (117.87 → 200.92), while monsoon months (July-August) show the cleanest air (~57-58). This seasonal swing suggests weather patterns and stubble burning heavily influence air quality.
  • PM2.5 and visibility are inversely linked by climate zone: IGP (Indo-Gangetic Plain) and Semi-arid zones show the highest PM2.5 levels with correspondingly lower visibility, while coastal/island zones (WestCoastHeavy, Island) show better visibility with lower particulate levels.
